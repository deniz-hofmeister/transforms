A. What I concede to you after the stress test
A1. Results stay labelled and owned: one same-time `Transform` plus `CrossTimeTransform`. The safety critic showed three things my unlabelled `Copy` result loses: a flattened result re-inserted under the wrong edge is rejected today by its own labels (ParentMismatch) and would be `Ok` under my draft; adapters could no longer tell a cross-time result from a same-time one by type; a swapped lookup stops being self-describing. I drop `Edge<'a>`; inserts take `&Transform` and batches `&[Transform]` as you propose.
A2. `compose` and `inverse` are named and fallible; no total public `Mul`. `t = t*t` reaches inf in 1024 products, so "valid for every value" is false for translations unless every operation checks finiteness.
A3. `BTreeMap` history stays. The deque's steady-insert win (3.3–4.8x) is real but out-of-order inserts regress badly (newest-1% of a 60k window 9–13x slower, uniform 115–230x) and the end-to-end lookup gain did not reproduce.
A4. `hashbrown` stays for the name index (BTreeMap index ~5–6x slower per probe at 1k frames).
A5. Target/source vocabulary on the mapping type; parent/child only for topology calls and their errors.
A6. Same-frame lookup of an unregistered, non-empty name stays the identity.
A7. Thresholded normalization, not an unconditional Newton step: the unconditional step breaks `a * identity == a` bitwise in 28.8% of cases and changes 45% of product bits. The band is 8*EPSILON on the squared norm (zero fixed-point failures in 1.6e8 samples per population; 4 EPS has no margin).

B. Where I hold, with the evidence
B1. `Rotation` replaces `Quaternion`. Your design keeps an open `Quaternion` with public fields and public `rotate_vector`, `slerp`, `normalize`, `norm`, `conjugate`, plus `QuaternionError`. `from_wxyz(0.7071,0,0,0.7071).rotate_vector(v)` silently scales; `slerp` on tolerance-edge inputs returns a norm off by 1e-6; and `Transform::rotation()` hands back the open type. A unit-by-construction `Rotation` (private fields; `from_wxyz -> Result`; `w()/x()/y()/z()`; `rotate(Vector3)`; `IDENTITY`) is not "another type": it is the same count, minus `normalize`/`norm`/`conjugate`/`slerp` and minus `QuaternionError`. `Transform::new` then cannot fail on the norm. It is also what makes your own "every successful transform preserves numerical validity" a property of a type rather than of every code path that touches a rotation. The invariant is the 8-EPS band, maintained after every product and slerp by the same normalization the constructor uses; inverse is conjugate divided by the squared norm (no sqrt; measured 0.66 EPS rms against an f128 reference versus 0.99 for 2.4's normalize-then-conjugate; identity law exact).
B2. Concrete `Timestamp(i64 ns)`; delete `TimePoint`, `TimeError`, `impl TimePoint for SystemTime`, the `<T>` on every public type, and the `std` feature. Evidence in stress-critic-time.md: (i) dimos's wire time is already signed ROS nanoseconds, so its `SystemTime` conversion disappears rather than appears; the only third-party `TimePoint` impl found is a tests-only mock in roslibrust, and its generic `RegistryError<T>` is the inferred obstacle to typed error handling there. (ii) With the default `Timestamp` the time-arithmetic failure inside a lookup is already unreachable; `TimeArithmetic`, `AccuracyLoss`, `check_interpolation_time` and `GetError::Interpolation` exist only for custom clocks. (iii) About 450 library lines and most of the 659-line tests/time_traits.rs go. (iv) Today's interpolation ratio `diff.as_secs_f64()/range.as_secs_f64()` is wrong by up to 2 ulp in about 36% of cases; integer nanoseconds divided as f64 is exactly rounded for spans below 2^53 ns. (v) Clock separation by type is real only across different `T`; no studied user defines a type per clock, and within one `T` a sim stamp and a wall stamp already mix silently. (vi) E0283 on all-static setups goes. (vii) Signed, because every ROS `(i32, u32)` stamp then converts totally (two of four hand-rolled conversions alias or clamp today); with `from_secs_nanos` and its inverse `as_secs_nanos` (floor semantics) in the core, because the dependents critic measured the naive outbound `/ %` on a negative stamp giving `(0, 3794967296)`. The strongest counter-argument is an invisible bare-metal user with non-decimal ticks (embassy at 32768 Hz loses up to 0.5 ns per stamp); `duration_since` already returns a nanosecond `Duration` today, so that user's interpolation is nanosecond-quantized already. Concrete-to-generic later is the cheaper direction to be wrong in.
B3. Per-operation error enums instead of one flat `RegistryError`: `LookupError` (UnknownFrame, Disconnected, NoData, OutOfRange, NoCommonTime, NonFinite), `InsertError` (structural and expiry variants, each naming its frame), `TransformError` (construction/compose/inverse). A lookup `match` should not have to mention `CycleDetected`. `covered: Option<(T,T)>` splits into two variants — the Option payload is exactly what the AGENTS.md invariant warns callers not to misread. Batch attribution is a wrapper `BatchError { index, error: InsertError }` rather than `batch_index: Option<usize>` repeated in five variants, which lets a single insert return a meaningless `Some`/`None` distinction. With sealed valid-by-construction transforms, insert needs no numeric validation at all, so no numeric variant remains on the insert surface (decode validates). Errors derive `Clone + PartialEq` (a wait loop can keep `Timeout(last_error)`; 2.4's cannot).
B4. Interned frames and a topology-first, allocation-free resolver ARE part of the v3 design (internal, no public id). You asked for a whole-query prototype including failed lookups; it exists: 372,996 `Ok` queries with max difference 0.0 against 2.4, single-hop exact lookups bit-identical 27,069/27,069; robot leaf-to-leaf 2.7–4.3x, siblings under a 16-deep dynamic chain 20–33x, unknown frame at 1k frames >1000x (66 µs → 44–92 ns), 3 allocations → 0 inside the resolver (the two result Strings come back with A1); interpolating multi-hop chains are a wash (slerp dominates). Error attribution needs a cold second walk on the failure path to name the same frame as 2.4 — kept. Failed inserts must not intern a slot.
B5. One application method: `Transform::transform_point(&self, p: Vector3) -> Vector3` (R p + t), nothing else. You delete `Point` and leave users to hand-write `rotation().rotate_vector(p) + translation()`; three of the seven slips the review lists for that are hand-math slips. This is not the checked path (it checks no frame) and I do not claim it is; it pins the convention in code, lets the SciPy golden vectors pin a crate path again, and lets `Vector3` lose its arithmetic operators. Free vectors use `rotation().rotate(v)`.
B6. `Registry::with_max_age(d)` and `Registry::unbounded()`, no `new`, no `Default`. Every studied user sets a window; nobody uses `new`. This is naming, not policy.
B7. Rename `remove_transforms_before` (now inclusive) so the boundary change is a compile error, e.g. `remove_samples_through`. This reopens the 2026-11-11 "name stays" ruling, which was taken under the compatibility constraint; flag it for the maintainer.
B8. Drop the `approx` trait impls from the public API (keep it as a dev-dependency). They couple the crate's semver to approx 0.5 for what is test support. I withdraw removing `thiserror` and `hashbrown`.
B9. No `std` feature and no `try_now`. `Timestamp::now()` in transforms_io is the mechanism of dependents' hazard 6 (a wall-clock "latest" succeeding on post-dated hops). The crate becomes unconditionally `no_std + alloc`; the feature matrix halves.

C. Your proposals I have not accepted
C1. Per-edge `set_max_gap` in 3.0. I agree the hazard is the largest one left and core-or-nowhere, and per-edge is the right granularity. I doubt the shape: the default stays unlimited, so the three studied users (who ingest arbitrary /tf edges and do not know per-edge rates) stay exposed unless they add configuration; the only precedent (dimos) was a per-query tolerance; the policy must be set after the first insert; and hole-aware `latest_common_time` is new complexity with a bad worst case. Every shape is additive later (a setter or a lookup variant, and a new variant on a non_exhaustive enum). My position: specify it in the design as the first 3.x addition, do not ship it in 3.0.0. What would change my mind: a shape whose default protects an unconfigured user. Do you have one?
C2. `lookup_latest`. The dependents critic argues for it (all three ROS-facing users build it, one wrong for seven releases, adapters hide the lock so their users cannot hold one guard across `latest_common_time` + `get_transform`). You and the scope critic reject it as exact on the public API. I list it as a maintainer decision with both arguments unless you see a decisive point.
C3. `try_now` and `TimePoint::as_seconds_lossy` removal fall away under B2/B9.
